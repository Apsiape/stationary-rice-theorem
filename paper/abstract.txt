Which macroscopic questions about a noisy lattice can an algorithm decide from its local rule? Fix dimension seven and one positive rational replacement rate. Every nontrivial property determined by the number of extreme translation-invariant stationary laws, all-law ergodicity, and the existence of a translation-invariant stationary law with nondecaying local covariance is undecidable. A stronger theorem retains the numbers of extreme translation-invariant stationary states with nondecaying covariance and with a Bragg atom at some nonzero wavevector. The same statements hold for finite-depth circuits of local quantum channels with fresh product ancillas, followed by whole-site depolarization. Under a stated published-source condition $C_T$, at another fixed rational rate the total-count cuts ``at most $r$'' and ``at least $k$'' are respectively $\Pi^0_2$-complete for every $r\ge1$ and $\Sigma^0_2$-complete for every $k\ge2$, in both categories. The scope has a decidable boundary: a classical translation-invariant stationary law attains the noise entropy floor exactly when the deterministic rule is constant.
